# Directional extrapolation study

## Question

Can an inverse model recover Kiselev parameters when the test points lie entirely
outside the parameter range used for training? This is stricter than a random split:
the model must cross an unseen outer boundary rather than interpolate between nearby
training samples.

## Protocol

- Generate a noiseless 41 × 41 grid over `k ∈ [-0.04, 0.04]` and
  `wq ∈ [-1.4, 1.4]`.
- Predict `(k, wq)` from `(Omega, lambda, delta_r)`.
- Hold out the lowest or highest 20%, 30%, and 40% of the complete levels of one
  target parameter. No held-out level occurs in training.
- Compare a histogram gradient-boosting model (HGB) with a scaled two-layer neural
  network (MLP), over five seeds.
- Run size-matched random controls at every test fraction.
- Report error, skill relative to the nearest training-boundary value, distance from
  that boundary, and the standardized inverse-map Jacobian diagnostics.

The boundary skill is `1 - model MAE / boundary MAE`. Positive values mean that the
model improves on simply returning the nearest observed boundary value; zero means no
extrapolation benefit; negative values are worse than that baseline.

![Directional extrapolation results](figures/extrapolation_study/directional_extrapolation.png)

## Main result

At the representative 30% holdout, random-test NMAE remains low (0.0207 for HGB and
0.0384 for MLP, averaged across both targets). Directional extrapolation is much
harder: the corresponding means rise to 0.1662 for HGB and 1.3415 for MLP. The MLP
mean is dominated by unstable extrapolation toward low `wq`.

| Held-out direction (30%) | HGB target NMAE | MLP target NMAE | HGB boundary skill | MLP boundary skill |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `k` low → high | 0.1781 | 0.1158 ± 0.0142 | -0.0341 | 0.3275 ± 0.0823 |
| `k` high → low | 0.1820 | 0.1345 ± 0.0089 | -0.0398 | 0.2313 ± 0.0507 |
| `wq` low → high | 0.2137 | 0.2991 ± 0.0069 | -0.2210 | -0.7089 ± 0.0393 |
| `wq` high → low | 0.1778 | 3.1376 ± 0.8094 | -0.0541 | -17.6041 ± 4.7990 |

The MLP provides limited, reproducible evidence of directional extrapolation in `k`:
it beats the boundary baseline in both directions. It does not do so for `wq`. HGB is
more stable, but never beats the boundary baseline in these four 30% tests. Therefore
excellent random-split performance should be described as interpolation, not as
evidence of general extrapolation.

## Distance and identifiability

Error increases strongly with distance beyond the training boundary. For the 30%
HGB runs, the Spearman correlation between target error and boundary distance is
0.990–0.992 for `k` and 0.942–0.948 for `wq`. Conditioning also matters, but its sign
depends on the direction because both the Jacobian landscape and the held-out path
change together. These correlations are descriptive and should not be interpreted as
independent causal effects.

## Limitations

- The data are synthetic, noiseless, and generated by the same forward model used to
  define the study domain.
- The MLP reaches its iteration cap in some folds. Its catastrophic low-`wq` result
  is reported rather than hidden; it demonstrates model and seed sensitivity outside
  support, not a universal neural-network limitation.
- Only two model families and one fixed hyperparameter configuration are compared.
- NMAE is normalized by the full simulated parameter range. Boundary skill supplies
  a stricter local reference for the extrapolated target.
- This experiment tests numerical recovery on the selected domain. It is not a claim
  that parameter combinations outside that domain are physically realized.

## Reproduce

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python -m bhhairml.experiments.extrapolation_study
```

The command writes point predictions, summary metrics, distance/conditioning
statistics, and PNG/PDF figures to `artifacts/extrapolation_study/`.
