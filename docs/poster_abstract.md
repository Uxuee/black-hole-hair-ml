# Poster abstract

We study inverse identifiability of analytic black-hole hair parameters from
leading-eikonal ringdown and geodesic observables. Physical-group validation
prevents repeated mode observations of one system from leaking across train and
test, while numerical Jacobians locate local degeneracy. Across 1,577 valid dense
Kiselev systems, five-fold grouped validation gives \(R^2(k)=0.992\pm0.001\) and
\(R^2(w_q)=0.994\pm0.002\), but sensitivity becomes poor near \(k=0\). Smooth
synthetic geodesic proxies reduce the mean absolute \(w_q\) error from 0.0225 to
0.0143 and improve local conditioning; these proxies are not physical ray-tracing
results. A public GW150914 GR posterior supplies an observational-scale toy
tolerance only, not a hair detection or modified-gravity bound.
